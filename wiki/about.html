About MAIS

Math for AI Safety (MAIS) maintains an open repository of mathematical problems aimed at reducing catastrophic AI risk. We connect safety researchers with mathematicians and human-directed AI provers, seeking results that support interpretability, alignment and cooperation between AI agents.

The repository is intended for researchers across institutions. It brings together problem statements, proposed solutions and reviews, with links to the safety questions that motivate the work.

Work so far

As of October 2026, the volunteer effort has assembled 92 open problems and received approximately 40 solution submissions. Submissions are proposals for review, not a count of verified solutions.

An AI-assisted audit identified a gap in the published proof of a bounded Löb theorem, relevant to provable cooperation, and produced a proposed repair.

Problems and research agendas

The problems are grouped into eight research agendas. Each agenda sets out the mathematical background and explains how results could contribute to AI safety.

A problem is listed as open when we do not know a solution after checking the literature. If you find an existing result that answers it, please send the reference. Problem, agenda and paper identifiers remain the same when titles change, so they can be cited consistently.

Contributing

  1. Read the problem statement and any existing discussion.
  2. Open an issue with the problem identifier to ask a question or describe work you plan to undertake.
  3. Submit a proof, partial result or numerical evidence. State what you have established and what remains unresolved.
  4. Describe any AI assistance and include enough detail for others to check the work.

AI assistance and verification

AI-assisted proofs may help accelerate this work, but proposed arguments still need review. Contributors should disclose how AI was used and distinguish checked results from claims awaiting verification.

We plan to combine AI refereeing and community review with Lean proof checking. Human oversight is needed to assess whether a formal statement captures the intended mathematical question and whether the result addresses the safety motivation.

Next priorities

Our next priority is to recruit questions from outside safety researchers. We want to help turn those questions into precise mathematical problems, obtain expert assessment of their safety relevance, and return checked answers to the researchers who can use them.

We are also developing the submission and verification process. We intend to track verified solutions, researcher participation and evidence that results are used in safety research.

Background

MAIS was founded by Lionel Levine, a mathematics professor at Cornell University and author of Math for AI Safety: An Invitation for Mathematicians.
